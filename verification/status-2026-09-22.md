## 1. Deliverable and architecture

A complete, correct, **executable** formal specification of AMQP 1.0 core (OASIS Standard, Parts 0–5)
in Lean 4, with a clause-level ledger that makes completeness and fidelity *measurable* rather than
asserted. Downstream work — Rust implementation, extraction, proofs against it — belongs to
TemperMint; this repository holds the specification and its evidence.

Two **independent** artefacts answer the same vectors: `amqp-spec` (the specification) and
`amqp-ref` (separately written, same layers). A differential compares them. Around that sit four
corpora, a clause ledger with dispositions and an ambiguity register, and four verification tiers:

| tier | question | instrument |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** | is the specification internally consistent? | theorems, invariants, interface conformance |
| **V2** | does it discriminate? | the authored and generated corpora, both artefacts |
| **V3** | is it faithful to reality? | recorded third-party vectors, **admitted** |
| **V4** | would the suite catch a wrong specification? | planted specification mutations |

The design claim worth stating plainly: **each instrument is blind to something the others are
not**, and this period produced the first experimental demonstration of it (§4.2).

## 4. Findings of general value

**4.1 One rule, many shapes: a claim's source must be the thing the claim is about.**
Roughly twenty instances surfaced this period, every one caught by an independent party within the
hour. The shapes are worth cataloguing because each defeated a different kind of care: a count used
as a set; **silence read as data** (a rejected file, a build behind a pipe); a statement **vacuous
where the work is**; a statement **universal where only reachable states are at issue**; a
**shadowed expectation** (a vector whose refusal comes from a different rule than the one it names);
a **false claim inside the ledger**; a **convention that decays** past the sites a correction pass
visited; a claim **true when written and false when read**; and the newest, a **summary standing in
for its subject** (a commit's `--stat` trusted over the commit's content).

**4.2 A differential cannot see what both artefacts share.** 38 refusals agreed across both while
naming no protocol condition at all — an omission invisible to every instrument that compares the
implementations *to each other*. A clause-derived model found it. One experiment settled the
complementarity: a dropped MUST that the differential missed across **68,120 compared vectors** was
killed by the sweep as 29 divergences, 21 of them the mutant's signature.

**4.3 A sequence corpus measures transitions; entry assignments are reachable only by *starting* in
a state.** Twenty cells hung on two transposed lines for exactly this reason, and neither the corpus
nor the differential could have seen them.

**4.4 A quantity a layer maintains and no rule consults cannot be witnessed by any vector.**
`peerCount`, `peerCredit`, `remote-outgoing-window`, `available`, `drain` are all in this position;
the invariant is the only instrument that can hold them, and each is declared as a scope exclusion
rather than left silent. Two defects among them are protected by *nothing else*.

**4.5 A proof can be a green light wired to a defect** — see the contract in §3.

**4.6 The isolation rule.** A vector's refusal must be attributable to exactly one rule, and the only
way to know is to disable that rule and watch the vector fail. Three instances, the sharpest a
*negative* vector that looked like evidence for the size check while the channel rule supplied the
refusal.

**4.7 Receipts beat summaries.** A build's job count, a run's row count, and a commit's content via
`git show <rev>:<path>`. Summaries — a `--stat`, a script's success message, a test's last line — are
derived views, and this period refused to trust them in four separate failures of mine (§5).

## 7. The AMQP server question

Asked today, and answered from the tree rather than from impression.

**What exists.** The protocol engine is executable end to end over octets: `decodeFrame` returns the
frame *and the octets it consumed* (a stream splitter, proved to agree with the frame's own `SIZE`);
the codecs turn octets into the `Submission` values the connection step consumes; and `step` is a
total function from state and submission to a refusal with a named condition, or to admission **plus
the octets to write**. Two independent implementations of all of that already run over a corpus.

**What is absent, and why.** No socket, listener or transport exists anywhere in `Spec`, `Ref` or
`Harness` — deliberately, since no test may touch the network and the specification is offline by
design. Time is not modelled, so while the empty frame a timeout sends is now handled, nothing
decides *when* to send one. Messaging (Part 3 — sections, annotations, delivery state) is **S5 and
unwritten**, so a server could negotiate, open sessions and links, transfer and settle *frames*, but
not interpret *message bodies*. SASL exists as far as the ANONYMOUS exchange. One link per session,
and ids are `Nat` rather than RFC-1982 serial numbers.

**How it would be built.** Not by putting a socket in the specification. `PLAN.md` §12 anticipates
it: *"vectors, the verdict schema and the comparison are implementation-agnostic, so any
implementation — Gallina, Rust, or a third party's — can be added as a **runner** rather than a
rewrite."* A server would be a new runner: a small harness owning the listening socket and the
per-connection loop, calling the same `step` and writing what it returns.

**A correction I made while writing this.** I first described such a server as "a path to V3". That
is wrong, and the reason is the one V3 is built on: **independence is in authorship.** A server
built from *this* specification shares its reading of the artifact, so its agreement would report
nothing about fidelity — it is a *consumer*, not third-party evidence. What it can legitimately be is
a smoke consumer that exercises the corpus vocabulary over a socket, and possibly a demonstration
that the runner interface is real rather than notional.

## 8. Decisions requested

**D1 — What to widen to next, and when.**
The plan's order is S4 → S5 → S6 → S7, with the slice closed before breadth is added. S4 is
mid-flight: its session/link machine works and its corpus is green, but three proof obligations and
the session invariants remain.
*Options*: (a) finish S4's proof work before any breadth; (b) run S5 (messaging) beside it, as the
plan's wave structure did for R2–R5; (c) build the socket runner first, outside the current layer
list.
*Default if unanswered*: (b), because S5 is the only item that unblocks **both** message-bearing
servers and the largest remaining clause family (Part 3), and the plan already sanctions running
waves beside the proof ladder.

**D2 — What counts as independent for V3.**
The plan says independence is in authorship and experience rather than in language, and that a second
prover is a cross-check rather than an oracle.
*Options*: (a) third-party capture only — someone else's implementation, recorded and committed;
(b) third-party *or* an independent modelling in another prover, admitted with a disclosure of what
independence it claims; (c) leave V3 blocked until a partner exists.
*Default if unanswered*: (a), and we state plainly that the tier is empty rather than filling it with
something whose independence we would have to argue for.

**D3 — The UTF-8 lemma.**
The narrowest-form law's last distance is a round-trip lemma for `String.fromUTF8?`/`toUTF8` that
neither core nor mathlib states.
*Options*: (a) prove it here from `fromUTF8?`'s exposed body (a project of its own — bytes and
UTF-8); (b) leave the law conditional on it, which is its current honest state; (c) attempt to
upstream it and consume it from the pinned closure.
*Default if unanswered*: (b), with (c) as a secondary — the law is assembled and states its one
hypothesis, so nothing is blocked while the lemma is absent.

**D4 — The single-link session.**
The session model holds one link's state, which makes `sessions.1` (link uniqueness) vacuous and
admits two cases the artifact does not forbid.
*Options*: (a) keep the one-link model for S4 and revisit when a slice gives it a subject;
(b) widen now to a link registry before messaging.
*Default if unanswered*: (a) — recorded as a reading, and revisited at the slice that needs it.

**D5 — Whether the server is wanted at all, and as what.**
*Options*: (a) not now — the deliverable is the specification, and a server is a consumer;
(b) a socket runner scoped as a smoke consumer and a demonstration of the runner interface;
(c) a server as a deliverable with acceptance criteria of its own.
*Default if unanswered*: (a), with (b) prepared as a plan-level item of a few hundred lines if
messaging (D1b) lands first — since without S5 it can carry frames but not messages.
