`Contracts/Codec.lean` states `RoundTripOnEncodedValues` and `NarrowestEncoding`. Proving them in one piece would be an all-or-nothing milestone whose progress is invisible until it lands, so the proof is specified as a ladder, each rung a theorem that stands on its own in `lean/Proofs/` and is accepted by the same gates as everything else (no `sorry`, no `native_decide`, `#print axioms` clean apart from the library axioms already disclosed):

* **R1 — fixed-width scalars.** null, boolean, ubyte/ushort/uint/ulong, byte/short/int/long including the small forms, float, double, decimal32/64/128, uuid, timestamp, char. No length fields are involved, so this rung reduces to byte-level lemmas: the big-endian writer and the reader's `takeBe` agree, two's-complement sign extension inverts, and the width is fixed by the line rather than by the payload.
* **R2 — variable-width families.** string, symbol, binary in both the 8-bit and 32-bit forms. Adds the length-prefix agreement and the narrow/wide choice, which is where `NarrowestEncoding` first bites.
* **R3 — compounds.** list8/list32/list0, map8/map32, array8/array32. Adds the size/count bookkeeping (the size field counting exactly what follows it) and the item loops, so the induction is over the value's structure at two levels: the compound and its items.
* **R4 — described values.** The descriptor-then-value pair, which composes R1–R3 and needs the reader's fuel generalised (`fuel ≥ octets remaining` rather than `fuel = the whole buffer`).
**R1 is where it stands, and the rung's shape is proved even though its claim is not.** Seven obligations are proved and named: `go_length` and `beOctets_length` (the writer's big-endian fold has the declared width), `mod_mul_base` (the base-256 decomposition `n % (256*b) = n % 256 + 256 * (n/256 % b)`, which was the obstruction the rung first reported and which falls to `Nat.div_add_mod`, the two truncation facts and `omega` — no induction), `go_foldr_value` with `bigEndianFieldValue` and `fieldValueRoundTrip` (the field-value law, stated in the reader's left-fold orientation because that is the direction the reader folds), and `Spec.ReadLaws.takeBe_eq_fold` in the specification itself: `takeBe` at a checked offset returns the fold of the buffer slice it consumed. `RoundTripFixedWidth` remains stated, and its three remaining obligations are named rather than hidden: the `drop`/`take` characterisation of that slice at the composition site, which the module documents as a library step nobody could name from the pinned core; the two's-complement pair for the signed widths; and the non-integer fixed-width cases, which need the same bridge once.

**R2 is landed to the same standard as R1: the shape proved, the claim stated.** Six obligations are proved and named. The narrow/wide rule is proved at both sides (`lengthWidthOf_narrow`, `lengthWidthOf_wide`) and is *stated as code* — `def lengthWidthOf payloadLength := if payloadLength ≤ 255 then 1 else 4` — so the rule is part of the rung rather than something a proof re-derives from whichever branch it unfolded. `two_pow_eight_mul` bridges the bound as the writer writes it to the bound as the field law reads it. Then the two halves of the length agreement: `lengthPrefixed_eq` (the writer emits the narrowest big-endian field carrying the payload's length, then the payload unchanged) and `lengthPrefixed_ok` (the emitted octets are the field plus the payload, so the field is the whole overhead), the second with **no hypothesis at all** because the size of the list does not depend on the check that admitted it — a condition the rung first carried and then removed, which is the same discipline as proving an identity unconditionally instead of assuming what it needs. `takeBe_beOctets` is the reader's half and the theorem that makes a length field *readable*: a cursor positioned after a header reads back exactly the value the header encodes, composing the consumed-slice identity with the field's value law.

`RoundTripVariableWidth` and its shape predicate remain stated, with three obligations named and none blocked: the writer's row choice agreeing with `lengthWidthOf` on the payload it actually has, the reader's length field feeding back into how many octets it takes, and the recovered payload being the writer's payload under `utf8Of` for the text families. One obstruction is recorded rather than papered over: the *success* form of the size half needs `Functor.map` on `Except.error` reduced, and this closure has no simp lemma for it — the same family as R1's Array/List bridge.

**R5 is the first rung that is not about agreement, and it reported the difference precisely.** R1 to R4 each proved an equality between a write and a read-back; R5 proves an inequality between two encodings of the same value, which is a statement about the choice rule. Three obligations landed: `widthChoice_le_of_fits` (any width the table offers that carries every quantity is at least the width the rule chooses — the whole order-theoretic content of "narrowest", which the agreement rungs never needed because agreements do not consult the choice), `widthChoice_narrow_iff` (the narrow width is chosen *exactly* when one octet carries everything, so the wide choice is **forced** rather than a preference the writer could reverse — which is what turns "narrowest" from a house style into a property), and `lengthPrefixed_canonical_size`.

What `NarrowestEncoding` still needs, and the ruling on it: **the contract keeps its global statement; the gap is reader-side lemmas, not a weaker claim.** The law compares the writer's canonical octets against an *arbitrary accepted* encoding, and the four rungs all reason about writer-produced octets. But the reader's dispatch is a function of the accepted buffer's first octet — the same table decision the writer consults — so a global proof can case on that octet and know which width the accepted encoding used, without any API change. What is missing is a set of lemmas about *what the reader consumed*, per family, derived from `takeBe`/`takeBytes` rather than from the writer's behaviour. Those are proof-internal and they compose into the global law, so the statement does not shrink and no hypothesis is added to it. The distinction the rung was asked to preserve is the one that matters: "this is per family" and "this family's reader lemma is missing" are the difference between a contract that shrank and a proof with one more rung to climb.

**The reader-consumption rung, and the induction that had to be generalised.** `foldl_be_bound` proves `bytes.foldl (fun acc byte => acc * 256 + byte.toNat) init < (init + 1) * 256 ^ bytes.length`, and the generalisation to an *arbitrary accumulator* turned out to be necessary rather than tidy: the loop carries one, so the nil case at `init` is the only base that closes the cons case — stating it at `init = 0` leaves the step unprovable. That is the same reason `takeBe`'s own value law needed the loop rather than its reversal, so the pattern is worth knowing twice. On top of it, `takeBe_lt`: a field's value is bounded by its own width, so a length read from a one-octet field is at most 255 and one from a four-octet field is below `2 ^ 32`. **The caveat, disclosed rather than buried:** `takeBe_lt` carries the cursor's own check as a hypothesis, because extracting it from `takeBe`'s success means inverting its refusal branch and that goes through `Functor.map` on `Except.ok`/`error` — the obstruction R2 and R3 recorded. Every composition site already has that check, so the hypothesis costs nothing at the point of use; it is a hypothesis all the same, and the module says so.

What `NarrowestEncoding` still needs is now short and family-by-family: for the variable-width families, an accepted encoding's field has width one or four and carries the same length, so `takeBe_lt` bounds that length by the width, `widthChoice_le_of_fits` gives `lengthWidthOf length ≤ w`, and the sizes add. The compounds and described values are the same argument with their own quantities and their own constructor octets. The law itself is untouched: no hypothesis, no weakening, the contract's statement.

The module also gained a section stating why these lemmas live there rather than beside the codec's other laws: they are about the *choice*, not about the codec's behaviour — which is the distinction the rung was asked to make visible, in the file a reader will open first.

**The first family is done, on the first attempt, and that is the evidence the generalisations were right.** `lengthWidthOf_le_of_field` applies `widthChoice_le_of_fits` at this family's single quantity with the membership obligation discharged by the singleton, and `variable_family_canonical_le` is the inequality the law needs here: an accepted encoding is a `w`-octet field plus the payload, the canonical encoding is the rule's field plus the same payload, and the rule's field is no wider — against the canonical size R2 computed. Nothing had to be re-derived to get there: `takeBe_lt` bounds the length by the accepted field's width, `lengthWidthOf_eq_widthChoice` moves between the two spellings of the rule, and `widthChoice_le_of_fits` compares the widths, with `omega` doing the only arithmetic. Three sessions of arguing about general forms — five rungs, the accumulator, the quantities list, "is this per family or is the lemma missing" — pay off in exactly this way: the fourth thing to be proved is composed from the first three rather than re-derived, and the composition is short enough to be read.

What remains for this family is reading the two facts off an accepted buffer — that a variable-width row's field has width one or four, and that the octets after the field are the payload the value carries — and that is a fact about the *table* rather than something the reader withholds: `Spec.Codec.dataDecl` names the row for an octet and `Generated.Oasis.encodingOf` carries the declared surface, so it is statable without an API change. Compounds and described values are next, at their own quantities plus their constructor octets, which is why `arrayOctets_ok_length` and R4's `described_overhead` already exist in the shape the comparison wants.

**All four families are done, and none of them needed a new general form — which is the answer to the question that took five rungs to settle.** `sizeWidthOf_le_of_field` is `widthChoice_le_of_fits` at the compound's two quantities, `compound_canonical_le` is the size inequality, and `described_canonical_le` follows from the two componentwise inequalities. All three went through on the first attempt.

Two observations in that are worth more than the theorems:

* **One theorem covers lists, maps and arrays alike**, and the reason is structural rather than clever: all three frame their body as a size field and then that many octets, and an array's element constructor lives *inside* the octets its size counts — so the constructor appears on neither side of the comparison. The size-accounting subtleties that cost the value corpus four defects, and that this plan treated as per-form arithmetic, collapse into one statement once the comparison is written at the right level.
* **The described case is a monotonicity of `+` that mentions no field width at all**, which is R4's prose claim — a described value consults no choice rule — appearing here as arithmetic: a family whose consumption lemma is purely additive *is* "adds no new choice". It is also the lightest of the three, resting on `[propext, Quot.sound]` with no `Classical.choice`.

So the two load-bearing general forms — `widthChoice` over the quantities a field carries, and `takeBe_lt` carrying the cursor's check as a disclosed hypothesis — covered four of four families, and the form that looked most like over-engineering at the time covered the case the brief had not considered. The remaining step is the case analysis on the accepted buffer's first octet, which has to reconcile the within-family inequalities with the law's quantification over all values: which family an octet selects and what widths the table offers are facts about the declared surface, and that the accepted size is the field plus the quantity is a fact about what the reader consumed.

**The reader-consumption facts are now lemmas, and the wall behind them is one wall.** `takeBytes_advances` proves that a payload read advances the cursor by exactly the octets it took and returns exactly that many, so requirement (iii) of the global step — that an accepted size is its field plus the quantity the family inequalities compare — is a lemma rather than an assumption, resting on `[propext, Quot.sound]` with the cursor's own check as its only hypothesis. What remains for the global case analysis is the dispatch: which family an accepted buffer's first octet selects and what widths the table offers, both facts about the declared surface.

**The disclosed hypothesis is one naming decision repeated rather than four independent weaknesses.** `takeBe_lt`, `takeBytes_advances` and every composition site in this chain carry the cursor's check, because extracting it from a success means inverting a refusal branch through `Functor.map` on `Except` — and that inversion is a wall rather than a gap: it is not that the fact is unprovable, it is that this closure's `simp` will not reduce the shape. So the same hypothesis appears wherever the same shape arises, and if it is ever worth removing, **removing it once is what pays**: a single lemma or a local `simp` set for `Except.map`'s ok case would retire the hypothesis at every site at once. Recorded as an opportunity rather than a debt — the law does not need it, and a rung that stopped to fight it would be trading a proved chain for a tidier one.

**The `Functor.map` wall is retired, and its real cause was not what five rungs said it was.** `Except`'s `Functor` instance is derived from its `Monad` instance, so a call site's `x >>= f` and `f <$> x` are class *projections* applied to `Except.bind`/`Except.map`: every case equation holds by `rfl`, none is a `@[simp]` lemma, and `simp` will not unfold a class projection to find one — so `by simp` reports "made no progress" on a goal whose proof is `rfl`. `Proofs/ExceptMap.lean` gives the simplifier the five reductions, and both disclosure sites retired their cursor-check hypotheses: `takeBe_lt`'s by `except_bind_error`, and `takeBytes_advances`' with **no new lemma at all**, because its refusal is an `ite` rather than a `do`-block. So part of what the ladder called a wall was not one, and the rung said so rather than claiming five victories. The doc comments were *replaced* rather than left beside the retired hypotheses, and the two lemmas that remain unused by those sites are kept with the scratch verification that shows where they are load-bearing: a positive `do`-block reduction needs `bind_ok` and `pure_ok` together, and the array constructor's `some <$> declInRange …` lookup needs the map case.

Two sites elsewhere name the obstruction and are now stale: `Spec.Codec.elementDecl?`'s `<$>` lookup, which is adoptable through `except_map_ok` and is the genuine map site, and `CodecRoundTripVariable.lean`'s "stated but not yet proved" paragraph, which the rung verified can be closed with `by_cases` and `except_bind_error` and which `lengthPrefixed_ok` had already made moot.

**The narrowest-form law's remaining step is now a rung boundary with an entry point.** Eleven theorems are proved in R5 — nine of them the family inequalities and their rule-level parents — and `NarrowestEncoding` stands as the contract states it, with the dispatch case analysis and the composition named as the two things left. The rung recorded the dispatch step's shape rather than starting it: the three things it must reconcile (which family an accepted buffer's first octet selects, what widths the table offers, and that the accepted size is the field plus the quantity), which of those is table data and which is the reader's arithmetic, and the first lemma to write — the variable family's reading-off step, composing `takeBe_lt`, `takeBytes_advances` and `lengthWidthOf_le_of_field` into "the accepted size is the row's width plus the payload's length, with the width one or four". It also records that the array case is the codec's one genuine `<$>` site (`Spec.Codec.elementDecl?`), reachable through `except_map_ok`, and that a module wanting it must *import* `Proofs.ExceptMap`, because a `@[simp]` lemma that exists is not one `simp` can see.

The judgement to stop there is the same one endorsed at every other boundary in this ladder: a named boundary is worth more than a half-written module, and a rung that begins without room to finish leaves the tree ungreen for whoever picks it up. The next order is the dispatch lemma first and the composition second, because the composition discharges the value law and wants the law's own shape settled before it exists.

**The session's sharpest methodological finding is one family with four instances: a check whose failure mode is silent agreement with what it checks.** In each case the check was written in a way that made it incapable of detecting the difference it existed to detect, and in each case it was caught by something *independent* of what it was checking:

* **A corpus that encodes the implementation's error.** The connection generator's `refused()` helper defaulted every refusal to `framing-error`, so the corpus encoded the wrong condition for every state refusal — which is why 150 step verdicts agreed across two artefacts while both were wrong. The corpus could not have caught it, because the corpus *was* the error.
* **A contract that pins the code's behaviour instead of the artifact's rule.** `slice-sasl-challenge-from-client`'s fourth step expected the condition the implementation happened to produce rather than the one the artifact defines, and the frame corpus's encode vector expected a non-canonical form derived from what the writer did rather than the table's narrowest-form rule. Both were found by owners fixing the code and watching a contract disagree.
* **A sweep that shows only refusals.** A permission sweep made entirely of refusals cannot distinguish a correct rule from an implementation that refuses everything — the two produce identical evidence. The session sweep now gives every rule probe its complement, and the six controls that resulted are the only probes that can tell the difference.
* **A probe that fails for a reason unrelated to the rule it tests.** The session sweep's flow frames omitted `next-incoming-id`, which the artifact requires once `begin` has been exchanged and which every state a flow can be sent from is past — so a flow cell refused for that reason would have read as a permission refusal. Found in the preparation, before anything ran against live code.

The general form is worth stating because it governs how every check in this repository is judged: **a check has value only if it can fail for the reason it exists to detect, and separately from the other reasons it might fail.** The four instances are that statement's four shapes — the check agreeing with the thing it checks, the check unable to distinguish its two outcomes, and the check failing for an unrelated cause. The remedies are correspondingly four, and all four are now in use: an *independent source* (the artifact, never the implementation), an *explicit expectation* (a prediction, since a record predicts nothing), a *control* (the case the rule permits, not only the case it forbids), and a *well-formedness precondition* (so the failure has one possible cause). The checks that caught all four were each independent of what they checked: the audit read the artifact's definitions, the register compared them against a chosen condition, the coder compared the table's rule against the writer's output, and the sweep's author read the artifact's own mandatory-field rule against their own frames.

* **Silence read as data, twice in one investigation.** A one-step exchange probe printed nothing, which was read as the step being admitted: the runner *rejects* a one-step exchange (`an exchange is a sequence of steps, not a single one`), so its silence was a rejected file. Then two corpus-mutation runs piped the build to `/dev/null`, the build *failed* (`no such file or directory`), and the corpus ran the unmutated binary — so its zero failures meant nothing at all, and the mutation that was supposed to die had never been built. The general form is worth stating because this is the shape most likely to bite again: **an instrument must distinguish "no finding" from "no run", and a report that says nothing is a report about the instrument rather than about the subject.** Its practical corollaries are two, and the second is the one that would have caught both instances: **a mutation run must show its build** — with the job count, which is what proves the binary changed — and **every run must state its extent**, the number of rows, vectors or probes that actually executed. What broke the deadlock was reasoning rather than inspection: the peer's input is identical between the two runs, so the outcome was *determined*, and a report inconsistent with a determined outcome indicts the instrument. That reasoning also disproved the harness-shapes-the-frame reading, from `ExchangeStep` having exactly three fields.

* **A statement vacuous in the case that matters.** The credit contract's first draft said a sender's credit equals what the receiver's pair leaves room for — quantified over `position = some p`, and therefore *silent* while `position = none`, which is exactly where an `attach` must set both sides of the equation to zero. The statement was not wrong; it could not fail where the work was, and attempting its preservation lemma is what exposed that. The stated form now carries a second clause — a link with no flow state has agreed nothing about the partner's quantities — which is the artifact's ownership sentence applied to the *absence* of a link, and the first clause is its specialisation.

**The family has a corollary, and the session sweep's author supplied it by applying the family to their own observer.** A check that *reports* a difference is itself a check, and it has the same failure mode: a flag that never fires is indistinguishable from a flag that always passes. So the sweep's per-family tally — each family's cell count and its refused/admitted split, with a family the model says behaves uniformly flagged SCATTERED when it does not — was **shown to fire by driving it with a synthetic run** whose every other unattached-handle cell admits, after which it reports the split and the flag; and the legitimately mixed state-permission family is left unflagged, which is the other half of the demonstration. That is the four remedies applied to the instrument rather than to the subject: an independently driven input, an explicit expected report, a control that must *not* fire, and a shape in which a firing has one possible cause. The reader of a sweep report should be able to see both that the flag can fire and that it does not fire on variation the model already expects — a flag with neither property is a green light wired to nothing.

**The dispatch step's entry point is landed, and it produced the ladder's first upstream obstruction.** `readVariable_consumes` is the lemma the boundary section named: the accepted size is the row's width plus the field's length, that length is below `256 ^ width`, and the value carries exactly the octets the length counts. It is composed from `takeBe_advances` (a successful field read advances by the field's width, now *derived* from the read's own success rather than carrying the offset's check), `takeBytes_advances` and `takeBe_lt` — **all three called with only the result of the read, and no cursor check appearing in any new statement.** That the diagnostic held is itself the evidence the earlier API is right: the rung was told that needing to supply a check would mean an upstream statement was wrong, and none was needed. On top of it, `readVariable_canonical_le` is the composition the dispatch wants, and `variable_owners_have_two_widths` establishes the table fact by `decide` over the generated encodings, which is the only way a table fact should be established here: nothing transcribed, and a table change moves the proof rather than the transcription.

**The obstruction, named rather than worked around: the text families cannot be closed in this closure.** The writer's payload for `string` and `symbol` is `text.toUTF8.toList` while the reader's payload is the octets `utf8Of` decoded, so comparing those families' canonical size against an accepted size needs *octets that decode to a text re-encode to those octets* — and core states no theorem about `String.fromUTF8?` or `toUTF8` beyond `String.toUTF8_eq_toByteArray`, batteries states none either. `binary` needs no such fact, so its size comparison is closed outright by this module's lemmas; the two text families need either that theorem from upstream or one proved from `fromUTF8?`'s exposed body. That is a different kind of gap from the arithmetic ones the ladder has met: it is a fact about the standard library that does not exist, and it is recorded where a reader will find it rather than discovered again.

What remains in the dispatch is now three facts, and the useful news is that two of them are work.

`readCompound_consumes` and `readArrayData_consumes` take the compound and array families apart the same way the variable family was taken apart, and the array case is worth noting for what it *avoids*: the accepted size is what the reader *measured*, so the element row's lookup is never inverted, which is why the `elementDecl?` argument through `except_map_ok` that the boundary section anticipated turned out not to be needed. `readValue_described` carries the classification as a hypothesis rather than recovering it from the result, with the reason stated: recovering it would mean ruling out the other five branches by showing no other reader returns a described value, five obligations bought for no use. `decodeValue_entry` is the bridge the law's statement needs — `decodeValue` *is* `readValue` at the whole buffer with the buffer's length as fuel, and the count it reports is the final cursor position.

**What the S4 contract will rest on, extracted from the artifact rather than summarised from memory — and a correction that was worth making.** The field-level MUST `When the handle field is not set, this field MUST NOT be set` appears five times, on `available`, `delivery-count`, `drain`, `link-credit` and `properties`; `echo.2` carries a sixth occurrence of the phrase in the MAY class and it is a *different* rule — a handle-less `echo` asks only for the session endpoint state to be echoed, which is the field's one conforming use, so refusing it would forbid exactly what it exists for. My own first version of this note listed the five fields as `link-credit`, `delivery-count`, `available`, `drain` and `echo`: **a count verified and a set inferred from memory**, which is the family above with a new shape — the extraction gave the number five and I supplied the members. The implementer checked the note against the ledger before acting on it and found the error, which is the only reason it did not reach a corpus.

The law itself is two ownership sentences that no keyword scan can see, which is why the ledger's phrase list was extended to catch them: `link-credit` says *"Only the receiver endpoint can independently set this value. The sender endpoint sets this to the last known value seen from the receiver"*, and `available` is its mirror for the sender. The link section states three more instances — *"Only the receiver can independently choose a value for this field"*, *"Only the sender can independently modify this field"*, *"Only the receiver can independently modify this field"* — so an endpoint never invents the partner's quantity, it echoes the last value it was sent. **That is the credit-conservation law in the artifact's own words, and it is now implemented and pinned rather than merely stated**: the session state holds this endpoint's own `deliveryCount`/`credit` and the partner's last reported `peerCount`/`peerCredit`, three checks are in the corpus (`delivery-count.2` for the sender's own current count, `delivery-count.3` for the peer's flow carrying exactly the count the view holds, and the ownership refusal for a sender's flow whose `link-credit` differs from the credit this receiver last sent) — and adding the ownership check *refused one of the implementer's own setup steps*, which had the sender granting credit it had no right to. A rule that catches its author's setup error is the evidence that it bites.

**The statement exists as of now, and it is statement-only on purpose.** `lean/Contracts/SessionCredit.lean` carries `SenderCreditInvariant`: in a state where this endpoint attached as the link's sender and a flow state exists, that state's credit is exactly `Position.creditFor` of the peer's last reported pair against this endpoint's own delivery-count — stated through the specification's own constructor so the two cannot drift apart. Its documentation records what discharges it (initialisation at `attach`, where the truncated right side is zero by the ownership sentence; preservation per transition, where a received flow *sets* the equation by construction and a transfer *preserves* it because both sides move by one, with the credit gate stopping the truncated subtraction from wrapping), records the three guards as corpus scenarios rather than restating them, records that the receiver's side has no state law and why, and records the status plainly: **true by construction rather than proved**, because the preservation lemma does not exist yet. The acceptance theorem lands in the file when it does — a contract that states a law and says which obligation discharges it is worth more than one that quietly assumes the law holds.

**The strengthened clause forced a lemma's statement to name its reachable states — and the first reading of that finding, by the implementer and then by me in this plan, was wrong.** With the guard tree reduced, one branch's lemma failed under the law: the partner-sender branch, recording the partner's count while leaving the position alone where there is none, so that a session in `position = none` with `peerCount = initialCount` violates the absence clause. That was read as a defect in the layer, and the plan said so. **It is not, and the verification is what settles it**: the branch records the partner's count *only* when this endpoint's role is `receiver`, and that role is written in exactly two places — our own `attach`, whose match on the role always yields a position, and `detach`, which clears the role and the position together. So role-receiver implies a position exists, the branch is unreachable with no position, and the law holds in every reachable state. **The law is fine, the layer is right, and the defect was the lemma's statement** — stated over an arbitrary session, which is stronger than the branch can see: over an arbitrary session the record does violate the clause, and it is the branch's own guard that makes that unreachable. The lemma now carries those restrictions as hypotheses, which is where they belong.

So the escalated test's answer has **three** categories rather than two: the law is too weak; the implementation is wrong; or the statement is stronger than its subject. A proof obligation stronger than the code's behaviour is evidence about the *statement*, not about the code — and the reflex to read it the other way is what both the implementer and this plan did before checking. The absence clause still earned its place twice over: it made the induction possible, and it forced a statement to say which states it is about, which it could not have done while the equation was vacuous wherever `position` was `none`.

**That vacuity finding and this one are a pair, and the pair is the general form.** A statement can fail to match its subject's reachable set in either direction: *vacuous where the work is*, so it cannot fail where it matters; or *universal where only reachable states are at issue*, so it fails where nothing is. Both were caught by attempting a proof rather than by reading a statement, and the remedy is one sentence in both cases — state the property over the states the subject actually reaches.

**The family's shapes and the corrections this plan has taken are one rule, and the implementer supplied the unification.** A claim's source must be the thing the claim is about: for a claim about the code, the code; for a claim about a run, the run's own row count; for a claim about a binary, its build's job count; for a claim about the artifact, the artifact. Five instances from a single session, each reading *something adjacent* to the subject and treating it as the subject:

* a handle-less-flow field list derived from an extraction's count, with the members supplied from memory;
* a "fifth defect" concluded from a failing statement rather than a failing implementation;
* a clause routed by hand and then doubted without opening the file;
* a probe's silence read as its outcome, where the file had been rejected;
* a zero-failure corpus standing behind a build that had failed under `/dev/null`, so the binary was never rebuilt.

**The operational forms are receipts**, and that is why the rule is checkable rather than merely prudent: a run reports its extent, a build reports its job count, a contract states a prediction rather than a record, and a check states what it is checking.
