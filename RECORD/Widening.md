## §24. The widening's contracts

**The decision the contracts rest on.** §23.1's conformance interface is *frozen* — `Contracts/EndpointConformance.lean` states the claim and imports neither its proof nor its acceptance, so that a statement whose proof does not yet exist cannot be quietly weakened to meet one. Reading the interface settles a question I had expected to be open: **the alphabet does not change.** `Contracts/Conformance.lean`'s `Input` is the event set, and the widening the S4 triage measured — link identity, the unsettled map, the sender's delivery bookkeeping, more than one link per session — adds *state*, not events. The frames the widened rules decide on are the frames the alphabet already carries; what is missing is a session model that remembers anything about them.

**So the interface extends by addition rather than by edit, and there is a contract that makes "extends" mean something.** `Contracts/EndpointConformance.lean` is not touched: R3's proof is *about* it, and a contract edited after its proof is a contract nobody can audit. A second statement is frozen beside it for the widened model, and the relation between them is stated as a theorem rather than implied — on the fragment the old model reaches, the widened relation agrees with the old one. Without that projection the word "extend" would be a claim about intent; with it, it is a claim about terms.

**The four parts, in the order they can be written.**

1. **The widened interface statement — re-frozen before the corrective implementation.** The original `45df1db` statement exposed four state-shape defects under the promoted corpus, implementation costing, and ledger review: the two endpoint handle spaces can both use handle zero; executable specification steps cannot import state declared in their consumer `Contracts`; a handle-less `disposition` cannot traverse a function-valued link registry; and a tag-less disposition cannot traverse completed entries in a function-valued unsettled table. `lean/Spec/WidenedState.lean` owns the dependency-neutral declaration-only state, including finite complete key indexes for both functional tables, and `WidenedEndpointConformance.lean` owns aliases and the relation over it.
2. **The projection theorem statement — unchanged from `45df1db`**, which is what licenses calling the result an extension: on the states the old model reaches, the widened relation agrees with the old one. `OldEndpointFragment` and `WideningProjectsEndpointRelation` retain their public types and meaning through the state-boundary corrections.
3. **The vectors for the 26 clauses — twelve carried.** The original eleven and their three input corrections are carried by the generator. Ledger review found that `staged-disposition-after-detach` leaves its delivery in progress, so it cannot catch an implementation that forgets completed unsettled entries. `staged-disposition-completed-after-detach` completes an unsettled delivery, detaches without close or error, applies the settling disposition, and then attempts to resume that id and tag. Against the committed pre-index reference, step 7 was observed admitted in the mapped session rather than refused as malformed. The specification at that commit cannot elaborate once this frozen amendment requires the new index field, so no synthetic hybrid is claimed as a second red observation; both artefacts give the pinned refusal after implementation. This twelfth boundary scenario is the failure-first contract for `disposition.4`'s completed case.
4. **The implementation and its proof — present as a new rung rather than a replacement.** The rung makes `Impl.Core.State` exactly `WidenedImplementationState`, proves `WidenedEndpointConforms` for the existing `Impl.Core` endpoint, and proves `WideningProjectsEndpointRelation` for the old fragment. The corrective cut adds the exact unsettled-tag index to both independent readings and applies a disposition's delivery-id range to every indexed live entry. R3 remains proved at its own frozen type. There is no adapter, second implementation, or second shipped-state shape.

**What this now settles.** There is one shipped core. The old Session link fields remain only for the restricted model frozen Settlement and SessionCredit contracts quantify over, and the widened codecs neither dispatch through nor synchronize them. Sixteen of the 26 clauses are pinnable and all sixteen now have complete structural or checked carriers. The ten the harness cannot pin remain named proof obligations in `Proofs`; a state-facet theorem or condition shadow is not the endpoint action or output frame the clause requires.
**The 26 clauses in the order their vectors pin them.** They are not 26 behaviours: read against the widened state they cluster into eight, and a vector pins several clauses each, which is how the corpus already works. The list below is the contract the vectors were authored from, with the clauses each one carries and what was observed failing before the model that satisfies it existed.

1. **A link is named, and its name identifies it** — `links.1` (names MUST uniquely identify the link among all links of the same direction) and `links.5` (an attach for a link whose terminus is already associated MUST include its unsettled delivery state). *Failing before*: the attach has no name to compare and no second link to compare it among, so the step is undefined rather than refused.
2. **Detach, re-attach, and resume are three different things** — `links.2` (attaching a suspended link rather than resuming it), `links.16` (a destroyed endpoint's peer MUST resume), `links.17` (the resume request is disambiguated from a pipelined re-attach), `links.18` (a pipelined attach MUST terminate the session with `errant-link`), `closing-a-link.2` (signalling closure by re-attaching and sending a closing detach). *Failing before*: no link survives a detach, so nothing can be resumed or mis-attached.
3. **The unsettled map on the attach** — `attach/field:incomplete-unsettled.u1` (an absent delivery's state is not inferable from an incomplete map), `attach/field:unsettled.1` (the local and remote delivery states for a delivery-tag MUST be compared to resolve in-doubt deliveries), `attach/field:unsettled.5` (the map MUST NOT contain null-valued keys), and `attach/field:unsettled.6` (on re-attach rather than resume it MUST be null). *Failing before*: there is no map to validate, so a null-valued key and a null map are indistinguishable from an absent field.
4. **An incomplete unsettled map latches the sender** — `attach/field:incomplete-unsettled.1` and `.2` (MUST NOT send new deliveries), `.3` (MUST detach and re-attach at least once). *Failing before*: with no sender-side record there is nothing for the latch to suppress.
5. **Resuming a delivery** — `resuming-deliveries.2` (a marked resend MUST be resumed), `.3` and `.4` (deliveries the receiver cannot settle MUST be ignored by the sender), `.5` (the rest MUST be resumed), `.8` (the resume flag MUST be set when resuming), and `transfer/field:resume.1`–`.3` (the receiver ignores resumed deliveries not in its map; the sender MUST NOT resume deliveries not in its own; a resumed delivery spanning several transfers MUST carry the flag on each). *Failing before*: neither side keeps the record the rules read.
6. **The two sides reduce unsettled state together** — `resuming-deliveries.6` and `.7` (reduce as much as possible, then suspend and re-attempt). *Failing before*: nothing to reduce.
7. **A delivery-tag is unique among the deliveries either end could still consider unsettled** — `links.23`. *Failing before*: no unsettled set exists to be unique among, which is what makes today's rule vacuous rather than wrong.
8. **Settlement survives until the link does not** — `disposition.4` (deliveries remain unsettled while the links are neither closed nor detached with an error). A disposition has a role and delivery-id range but no handle or tag, so it ranges over every registered link with the matching local role and every indexed unsettled entry on those links, including detached links. *Failing before*: the in-progress case is carried, but a completed delivery has no reachable tag in the function-valued table; the disposition therefore leaves it resumable.

**The adjacent credit-vector correction has its own failure-first record.** Before its second
delivery received a distinct tag, `exchange-link-credit-regranted` and
`staged-link-duplicate-delivery-tag` required opposite verdicts for the same tag-reuse input; the
only intervening difference was a flow granting credit, which does not settle delivery zero. The
widened model correctly refused the positive vector under `links.23`, leaving one corpus mismatch.
Changing only delivery one's tag preserves the credit scenario and the uniqueness carrier.

**The transaction-corpus correction is also failure-first, not a model exception.** The restricted
one-link model ignored attach names, so the mismatch stayed invisible. The widened registry
correctly created two identities; the following flow then named a link whose local endpoint had
never attached and was refused by the delivery-count rule. Eighteen of the 36 transaction verdicts
failed per artefact. The encoded peer frames remain byte-for-byte unchanged; changing only each
mismatching structured send attach's name to its peer's name preserves valid frame sizes and the
intended one control link. Reading delivery-count session-wide would contradict the rule's link
endpoint subject.

The same pass found a separate invalid role pair in
`txn-payload-on-a-link-that-is-not-a-control-link`: both endpoints attached as receivers before the
peer sent a transfer. Both widened readings correctly refused it. Its peer attach is rebuilt through
the frame writer as the sender, including the sender's mandatory initial delivery count; no encoded
length is hand-maintained, and the non-control-link payload verdict remains admitted.

**What the vectors are not.** Every one of these is a *behaviour at the harness boundary* — a vector the runner replays, as the corpus's scenarios are — and none is a proof obligation. The proof obligations are §24's projection theorem and the widened instance's own; if a behaviour here needed a theorem rather than a vector, the widened model would not be reachable by the corpus, which would be a finding about the model rather than about the vector.

**The readings the vectors rest on, settled when the failure-first vectors were authored.** Three rulings the widening vectors now carried in `vectors/generated-exchanges.ndjson` were written against, recorded here so the corpus's notes cite this paragraph rather than re-arguing it. Promotion into the carried corpus is complete; the failing-before verdicts remain their evidence until the widened model lands. One correction came with them: behaviour 3's citations were keyed wrong against the artifact — "an absent delivery's state is not inferable" is `attach/field:incomplete-unsettled.u1` (unkeyed, `deferred:S4` in `unkeyed-normative.json`), the null-key prohibition is `unsettled.5`, the null-on-re-attach rule is `unsettled.6`, and the incomplete-map family has no `.4`, the detach-and-re-attach obligation being `.3`. The count was right and stays: the twenty-sixth clause is `u1`, which the miskeying had hidden, and the promoted vectors cite the keys whose text they pin.

1. **The condition and reason class each refusal names.** The layer's own vocabulary decides each by the rule it already applies — `invalidField` for a field that breaks its own rule (a conditionally required field's absence included, as `attach/field:initial-delivery-count.1`'s missing sender count already is), `illegalStateCondition` for a well-formed frame at a forbidden moment (as a transfer offered by the link's receiver already is):
   - `links.1`+`links.5` — a second attach for a link whose terminus is associated, no `unsettled` field: **`amqp:invalid-field`, class `malformed`.** A conditionally required field absent, the same shape as the initial-delivery-count refusal.
   - `unsettled.6` — a re-attach carrying a non-null map: **`amqp:invalid-field`, class `malformed`.** The field's value breaks the field's own rule.
   - `incomplete-unsettled.1`+`.2` — a new delivery offered while the peer's map is incomplete: **`amqp:illegal-state`, class `illegalState`.** Every field on the transfer is legal; the latch makes the moment forbidden. The send is not taken and the refusal reports the clause's condition, as the layer's other send-refusals do.
   - `links.23` — a delivery-tag reused while a delivery holding it is still unsettled: **`amqp:invalid-field`, class `malformed`.** A rule on the tag's value, the same reading as `transfer/field:delivery-tag.u1`'s continuation mismatch.

   `links.18` keeps the artifact's own **`amqp:session:errant-link`, class `illegalState`** — a session termination, placed as the handle-in-use refusal already places its session-error.
2. **What no vector pins.** The corpus observes per-step verdicts on frames offered and received; it cannot observe a spontaneous emission or a held comparison, and no vector pretends otherwise. `links.2`'s second half (the first attach closed with `amqp:link:stolen`): **no vector** — the emission follows a receive, and no step asserts it; the `links.1`+`links.5` family's admitted counterpart pins the stealing attach itself. `closing-a-link.2` (the partner's re-attach and closing detach): **no vector** — both halves are emissions, and the disposition's note already records the frame-construction half. `attach/field:unsettled.1` (the comparison): **no vector** — held, not emitted; its observable shadow is the receiver ignoring a resumed delivery absent from its map, which the `resume.1` vector pins. `attach/field:incomplete-unsettled.u1` (absence is not evidence of settlement): **no vector** — a rule about what the recipient may *conclude*, not about a frame. `resuming-deliveries.5`/`.8` (the sender MUST resume, flag set): **no vector** — a MUST-send the corpus cannot see; the receiver-side shadow (the flag required on the first transfer) is `resume.3`'s staged vector. `resuming-deliveries.6`/`.7` (reduce, then suspend and re-attempt): **no vector** — obligations over emissions; a peer that never reduces violates nothing a per-step verdict can name. `attach/field:incomplete-unsettled.3` is the one emission clause pinned **through its consequence**: the latch vector's second half shows a new delivery *admitted* after a detach and a complete re-attach, which is "at least once" read as the latch's lift; the obligation's emission half stays unobservable and the note says so. For each no-vector clause the behaviour's note names the clause and this reason, and the ledger's S4 entries carry the same record when the model lands.
3. **`links.5` and `unsettled.6` partition by the link endpoint's life, and no frame falls under both.** The artifact's own definitions make three cases of an attach the name+direction identifies: the link **attached** (endpoint live, handles mapped) — a second attach is `links.2`'s stealing case and `links.5` governs: the unsettled delivery state MUST be included, read as a non-null map (null carries no state; absent and null refuse alike under ruling 1); the link **detached** (endpoint live, no handles — what `detach(closed=False)` leaves) — a re-attach, and `unsettled.6` governs: the map MUST be null; the link **suspended or destroyed** (termini outlive endpoints; `detach(closed=True)` or a link error) — a resume, and `links.5` with `links.17` govern: non-null unsettled, whose absence is the pipelined re-attach `links.18` terminates for. The tension dissolves because "reattaching" in `unsettled.6` names the detached-endpoint case only, while `links.5`'s "already associated with a terminus" is read of the terminus the attach would associate a *new* endpoint with — which is what the link-name identity makes visible.


**And ten of the 26 cannot be pinned by a corpus step at all, which is a finding about the harness rather than about the clauses.** Each requires the endpoint to *emit* a frame with a chosen content, a MUST-send, or a comparison nothing reads — while the corpus observes per-step verdicts over send and receive steps, where a send step's value is the vector's own and the session layer reports no octets that a refusal writes. The error symbol a model would choose is therefore unobservable: a model can satisfy all ten and no corpus step can say so. They remain named proof obligations rather than acquiring a second untestable-list vocabulary. `Proofs` establishes each state facet the widened model can express and names every required action it cannot; an unrepresented emission stays deferred, and a theorem about its desired condition is not counted as a theorem that the endpoint emitted the frame. Naming that boundary is the difference between a widening that looks covered by 26 vectors and one covered by sixteen. The authoring slice found this while writing the frames, reported it as a harness boundary rather than working around it, and recorded each clause's partial treatment in the note of the vector nearest it.

## §26. The widened model's shape

**Re-frozen in `lean/Spec/WidenedState.lean` and `lean/Contracts/WidenedEndpointConformance.lean`.** The original state declaration in `45df1db` had one handle-keyed link table. The promoted corpus proved that shape impossible: local and remote endpoints may both use handle zero, and either handle may move while the logical link survives. A second review found that executable `Spec` steps over a `Contracts`-owned state would invert the repository's dependency layer. Costing the disposition arm exposed that its handle-less range needs a finite link-registry index. The ledger review exposed the corresponding inner problem: a disposition also carries no tag, so completed entries in a function-valued unsettled table need their own finite exact index. Both indexes carry keys only; neither is a second source of protocol state.

**The shape.** `WidenedProtocolState` holds the connection and channel-indexed widened sessions. `WidenedSession` holds the restricted old session for its frozen contracts, a stable registry `links : LinkId → Option WidenedLink`, its duplicate-free exact traversal index `linkKeys : List LinkId`, and independent `localHandles` and `remoteHandles : Nat → Option LinkId`. Each `WidenedLink` holds one shared `unsettled : DeliveryTag → Option UnsettledDelivery` table and its duplicate-free exact `unsettledKeys : List DeliveryTag` index. `LinkId` is the uninterpreted name plus the local role. `LinkNamesUnique`, `RegistryKeysComplete`, `UnsettledKeysComplete`, and `HandlesResolveLinks` make key integrity, exact traversal, and directional resolution part of `WidenedProtocolStateValid`.

| state | the obligations it carries |
|---|---|
| `LinkId.name`, `LinkId.localRole`, stable `links` registry | `links.1`, `links.2`, `links.5`: identity survives handle reassignment and is unique by name and local direction |
| exact duplicate-free `linkKeys` index | finite traversal for handle-less session operations; disposition visits every matching-role link without making the index a second source of link state |
| `localHandles`, `remoteHandles` | the two endpoint-local handle spaces; the same numeric handle can resolve independently in both directions |
| `WidenedLink.localEndpoint`, `.remoteEndpoint` | independent terminus association, `LinkLife`, and incomplete-map latch for the two endpoints; `links.2`, `links.5`, `links.16`–`.18`, `closing-a-link.2`, `incomplete-unsettled.1`–`.3`, `disposition.4` |
| one `unsettled : DeliveryTag → Option UnsettledDelivery` plus exact duplicate-free `unsettledKeys` | `unsettled.1`, `.5`, `.6`, `incomplete-unsettled.u1`, `resuming-deliveries.2`–`.8`, `resume.1`–`.3`, `disposition.4`, `links.23`; one table is a shared history, and the key-only index lets a tag-less delivery-id range visit completed entries |
| `position`, `peerCount`, `peerCredit` | the existing per-link credit arithmetic preserved when the session-wide slot is removed |
| three settlement-mode flags | the existing negotiated sender and receiver settlement obligations preserved per logical link |
| `WidenedDelivery`, `deliveryTag`, `deliveryFormat` | the in-progress delivery and continuation checks; `WidenedDelivery.resumed` distinguishes a delivery whose first transfer carried the resume flag from one whose continuation first claims it |
| `nextDeliveryId` | `resuming-deliveries.2`–`.5`, the sender's record of what it sent and from where |
| `transactions` | the existing control-link state preserved on the link it belongs to rather than left as a session-wide slot |

**Five choices worth naming.**

1. **The two handle spaces share one logical link and one unsettled history.** Two link records would permit the differential's two readings to agree while local and remote handles silently accumulated different unsettled maps. The registry and the single tag-keyed table make that divergence unrepresentable.
2. **`UnsettledDelivery` holds both endpoint states, not one.** `attach/field:unsettled.1` says the local and remote states for a delivery-tag must be *compared* to resolve an inconsistency, so each entry stores `localState`, `remoteState` and the sender's `deliveryId`. One state per tag would make the clause unstatable rather than satisfied.
3. **Tag uniqueness means one tag denotes one delivery, not that the tag can never be retransmitted.** The tag-keyed function cannot represent two delivery ids at one tag; a resumed transfer carrying the same id and tag addresses that existing entry. This is the boundary between the conforming resume vector and `staged-link-duplicate-delivery-tag`.
4. **The link name is a `String`, and the model does not interpret it.** The standard gives a link name no grammar, and routing is a node's business outside this repository. A name is compared and stored, nothing more.
5. **A handle-less and tag-less disposition ranges over every matching-role link and every live
   delivery entry on those links.** `linkKeys` makes the registry finite and traversable;
   `unsettledKeys` does the same for each functional table. Their exactness invariants prevent
   missed or ghost links and deliveries, and neither order has protocol meaning.

**What the shape deliberately leaves out.** No application interface: the widened model decides *when* a resend is permitted and *what* the frames must carry, and never asks what the application wanted to send. No durability: `disposition.4` says deliveries stay unsettled while the links live, and the link's life is the session's, which is the process's. And no receiving-side application state beyond the map — a delivery absent from it is one the clause says nothing can be inferred about, which is why `attach/field:incomplete-unsettled.u1`'s unkeyed sentence is a rule about *not* consulting a default.

**How the 26 land on this shape.** Each is one of three things, and the fold is what the implementation and its proof will be measured against: **structural** (the representation cannot express the violation — uniqueness, and the null-key prohibition which a tag-keyed function already satisfies), **checked** (a guard the step functions read, which is where most of the 26 sit), and **still deferred** (a rule whose subject the widening does not reach, to be named rather than silently carried). The three-way classification is a planner act like the dispositions themselves, and it is recorded in the ledger only when each carrier exists rather than inferred from the frozen state contract.
