**Acceptance, checked against the tree rather than against intention, and three parts were outstanding at that reading — one of which the paragraph below records as since discharged.** The slot's line names four things and the contracts line names four files; after them, a coder can be told what is actually here. **What stands**: credit conservation is proved, not merely asserted — `lean/Proofs/SessionCredit.lean` carries ten theorems around `SenderCreditState.invariant`, with the initial state, the frame step, the fresh link, the flow step and the attach case each named, so the invariant is preserved by the operations rather than restated over them; `drain` and `echo` are modelled in `lean/Spec/Session.lean` at the two `flow` clauses that couple the link's fields to the handle; and the window state the arithmetic ranges over is the type `lean/Contracts/FlowControl.lean` states. **One of the three is now closed, and the way it was closed is the finding.** `SessionWindows.lean` did not merely lack citations — four of its five were to the *wrong sentences*: the received-flow adoption attributed to `session-flow-control.4` is `.7`/`.8`, the received-transfer decrement attributed to `.5` is `.4`, and one clause was stated twice with a different index each time. It is retired and `FlowControl.lean` carries the laws with registered ids, which is the repository's rule applied to a corrected claim: the corrected version replaces the superseded one rather than sitting beside it, because a reader would reasonably trust the second copy. That a resolving-id gate cannot catch this class is now written into the citation gate's own header, since it is the thing that would otherwise be read as coverage: `s0_vector_citations.sh` resolves ids and does not compare a citation against what the clause says. **Handle uniqueness is proved for the operation that allocates, and three ties are owed.** `Proofs/HandleUniqueness.lean` carries `attachLink_handle_uniqueness` over an invariant of the two registries — both `Nodup`, and each held handle present in its registry — so the operation that allocates can no longer allocate twice. What is *not* yet claimed is preservation by every operation: `detachLink`, `flowLink` and `transferLink` preserve the invariant but their do-block ties are not discharged, and the module gives each one's residual goal verbatim — for `detachLink` an `if` whose *both* branches succeed, which is why the refusal-shaped chain lemmas do not match it, with `by_cases` on the named condition or a `split` reaching an `if` head inside the do-block as what it would take. That was attempted and refused precisely rather than left vague, with a lemma verified in isolation yet reported *unused* at the site, which localises the difficulty to how that condition elaborates rather than to the lemma. The acceptance line asks for the invariants *proved*, and it is met for one operation of four; this paragraph says so rather than implying the stronger claim.

(`flow-negative-continuation-delivery-id-differs`, `-delivery-tag-differs`, `-message-format-differs`), and `tests/contracts/s4_flow.sh` replays that corpus against *both* executables requiring zero failures — so the family the probes found
uncarried is carried by both artefacts and pinned by a gate, which is what the delegation asked for. **Of S4's outstanding items, this one closes; the three handle-uniqueness ties and the interleaved-deliveries restriction stand**, the first as
proof work in `Proofs/HandleUniqueness.lean` with each residual goal given verbatim, the second as a consequence of `MODEL RESTRICTION: the S4 slice contains at most one link per session`. **And the three handle-uniqueness ties are discharged, so that item closes too** (`7152082`): `detachLink`, `flowLink` and `transferLink` each preserve the invariant, proved

accepted theorem (no `Classical.choice`). **The obstruction was join points rather than a missing lemma**, and the search that closed it was measured rather than guessed: `cases` has to sit inside the reduction loop and

A peer that **interleaves two deliveries** on one link is admitted as well, and that one is *not* a missing check: `links.31` constrains the sender, and the model's single held delivery cannot represent the second — so the second delivery's first transfer is absorbed as a continuation and keeps the *first* delivery's id. The two zero-length splits are admitted too, which is nothing being forbidden rather than a gap, and the interior sweep 1..N-1 remains the family's positive set.

**The adequacy control is delivered.** `vectors/flow.ndjson` holds 34 vectors fragmenting one 30-octet message: the whole message in one transfer, **all 29 interior split points** as two-transfer framings, three three-transfer framings at chunk boundaries, and a continuation repeating the first transfer's fields. The two negatives are a transfer offered after the message ended (`amqp:invalid-field`/`malformed` from both artefacts) and a fragment one octet past the announced frame size (`amqp:connection:framing-error`/`limit`, where the same split is admitted at the limit). `tests/contracts/s4_flow.sh` pins the split count, the pair's non-vacuity and every refusal's condition, and passes with 188 admissions and 2 refusals.

**Every step in that family is a `receive` step, deliberately**: the octets are the vector's and the readers are the two artefacts, so neither wrote the fragmentation it is read against. A `send` step would have the artefact produce the fragmentation and the family would be checking a writer against itself.

Both files the contracts line names now exist: `lean/Contracts/FlowControl.lean` carries the window laws and `lean/Contracts/Settlement.lean` the disposition rules — the latter recording that the disposition's mechanics have no keyed clause at all, so those rules are stated as readings of the field documentation rather than given a citation they do not have.
